For Students & Interns

If you're starting research with me — usually on deepfake detection and the harder problem behind it, building models that know what they don't know — this is the path I point you to and the resources I'd hand you on day one.

The problem

Deepfakes are synthetic or manipulated media; detection is the task of telling real from fake. Getting high accuracy on one dataset isn't the hard part — generalization is. A detector trained on one kind of manipulation tends to fall apart on manipulations, cameras, and compression levels it hasn't seen. How calibrated and how robust to adversarial stress a detector is rounds out the picture.

Keep three words in mind the whole way through:

GeneralizationCalibrationRobustness

The north star. The goal isn't just a classifier. It's an agent — a system that perceives media, reasons about it, and flags what it can't vouch for. You don't build that in week one, but everything on the path below points toward it.

↓ The learning path — read it as a sequence
01

Foundations — classical machine learning

The gentle on-ramp, each a few lines in scikit-learn: linear/logistic regression, SVMs, random forests. These teach the vocabulary everything else builds on — features, decision boundaries, overfitting, ensembles — and the evaluation metrics that matter, especially AUC and why accuracy alone misleads on imbalanced or shifted data.

scikit-learn: getting started → User guide →
02

Deep learning & the architecture ladder

Write one raw PyTorch training loop by hand so the mechanics aren't a mystery, then switch to PyTorch Lightning — the actual stack — which removes the boilerplate. Learn what a convolution does and why CNNs suit pixels. Then climb the ladder as orientation: attention → transformers → world models → frontier video models. The goal is seeing how the pieces connect, not mastering transformers in a day.

PyTorch 60-minute blitz → PyTorch Lightning → The Illustrated Transformer → World Models → 3Blue1Brown: Neural Networks →
03

Generative models & how fakes are made

You can't understand detection without understanding generation. Three families: VAEs (the classic face-swap is an autoencoder trick), GANs (whose discriminator does exactly your job — real vs. fake), and diffusion (today's best image and video generators). Then how those become deepfakes — face-swap, reenactment, fully synthetic video — and why a detector good at one is often blind to another.

VAE explainer → Illustrated Stable Diffusion → FaceForensics++ → DeepfakeBench →
04

Uncertainty, calibration & robustness — the core

This is the heart of the work. Calibration: do the confidence numbers mean anything? A model that says 90% should be right about 90% of the time. Uncertainty quantification: does the model know when an input is unlike anything it trained on? "Confidently wrong on novel inputs" is the failure mode we most want to avoid. SNGP — the method EQUINE builds on — spectral-normalizes the hidden layers and swaps in a Gaussian-process head whose variance grows as inputs drift from the training data, which is what lets a model effectively say "I've never seen anything like this."

On Calibration (Guo et al.) → SNGP overview → EQUINE (runnable notebooks) → Reliability survey →
05

Agentic AI

Where the path leads. A task is one model call; a workflow is several calls on rails you define; an agent directs its own steps and tools toward a goal. The building block is the augmented LLM — a model plus tools, retrieval, and memory. Tie it back to the north star: your eventual detector as an agent that looks at media, reasons, and flags what it can't vouch for.

Tools & good habits

The fastest way to pick up good engineering is to build it into the repo from day one and let it run automatically.

  • Modern Python stack — uv for environments and dependencies, PyTorch Lightning for training, and Ruff + Codespell wired into a pre-commit hook so quality checks run on every commit.
  • Run models locally — Ollama with Gemma for anything you don't want to send to a cloud service. Public research data and public code are fine for cloud tools; anything sensitive stays on your machine.
  • Use AI tools well — Claude Code and friends are fast drafters, not oracles. Write clear, specific prompts and always verify what they produce. This habit matters more the more you rely on them.
  • Repo hygiene — small, descriptive commits; a real README; a .gitignore that never includes data or model weights; one experiment = one config.
  • Keep a lab log — every run gets a dated entry: what you ran, the settings, the numbers, what you concluded. It's how the final write-up gets written without a last-minute scramble.
How we work together
  • Getting unstuck — try it yourself, then ask your tools or search, then ask me. A good question after a genuine attempt is exactly right; spinning for hours is not.
  • Read actively — for any paper, send me three sentences: what problem, what idea, what result. That habit is worth more than reading twice as many papers.
  • Write throughout — the final report grows as you work, so by the end you're revising, not scrambling. Concise and grounded: claims tied to evidence, no filler.
  • Scope deliberately — we choose your project once you have the foundations, so the decision is informed rather than guessed.
